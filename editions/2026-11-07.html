Saturday edition · Frontier safety day

GPT-6 Cyber goes Critical — and METR says Anthropic is improving 1.9x faster

OpenAI named GPT-6 Cyber on Nov 5 — a cybersecurity-specialized model rated “Critical” under its Preparedness Framework, gated behind approved Daybreak Red access — on the same day independent evaluator METR reported Anthropic's model-improvement pace running ~1.9x its pre-October-2025 rate. A strong paper day underneath: Harness-S (agent training), the SIMPLE inference-energy law, and a sharp result showing long-context “rot” is driven by conflicting entries, not length.

Editor's note: Both headline stories landed on Thursday, Nov 5. OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber is the most capable offensive-security model it has shipped, and the first it has walled off this tightly; METR's assessment is the first independent, inside-the-lab measurement of AI accelerating AI R&D. All of GPT-6 Cyber's exploit counts and scores are OpenAI-reported — see the caveat on brief 1.

The paper day is strong underneath. Harness-S trains agent policies on trajectories rescued by “sibling” harnesses so the capability transfers back to the base harness; the SIMPLE law gives the first closed-form prediction of LLM inference energy; and “Length Is Cheap, Competition Is Not” shows retrieval collapse is driven by rival entries competing for attention, not by context length.

Robotics had a day too. Open-HumanWAM open-sources a 50,000-hour human-video pretraining recipe with a stated scaling law, and SLA adds spatial alignment to a pretrained VLA policy without changing its inference architecture. TurnWise — the most-upvoted paper of the day — brings turn-level credit to streaming video RL.

Read the caveats before citing. METR stresses significant uncertainty in its ~1.9x figure, and Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative. Three briefs in this edition rest on secondary coverage only and carry no source links, by design.

⚠ Caveats — how to read this edition

  • GPT-6 Cyber figures are OpenAI-reported: all exploit counts, benchmark scores, and cost figures in brief 1 come from OpenAI; no independent audit of the Linux kernel run was available at publication time.
  • METR uncertainty: METR stresses significant uncertainty in its ~1.9x acceleration estimate (95% CI 1.5–2.8x), and Anthropic redacted “a majority of the evidence we found most informative,” so the public is asked to trust the evaluator's judgment more than the data. METR also notes AI is “not yet able to make highly significant and fully autonomous contributions to the most important parts of the model training process.”
  • Secondary coverage only: brief 3 (Claude Personal Finance), brief 6 (SemiAnalysis GDPx, via Gadgets 360), and the Cat Wu move in brief 7 rest on secondary coverage — no primary source links are shown for them, and none were invented. Briefs 4 and 5 likewise carry no source links.
  • Fresh preprints: all 12 papers are recent arXiv submissions with author-reported results, not independently replicated.
