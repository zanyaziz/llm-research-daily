Monday edition · A looped flagship, a proposed stop line & the open-weights weekend

Z.ai loops its 750B flagship twice — as OpenAI's chief scientist proposes a shared stop line for frontier training

Z.ai's GLM-5.4 continues training from its roughly 750B-parameter GLM-5 base and runs 28 shared layers twice per forward pass — depth without new parameters, a 1M-token context, and vision added to the GLM line for the first time. It is live now for GLM Coding Plan subscribers, with standard API access and open weights still pending and no benchmarks for the model itself yet. The same weekend, OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published the lab's internal perplexity curve — GPT-3.5 ≈ 0.99, GPT-4 ≈ 0.91, newer models below 0.8 — and proposed that labs agree on shared limits they will not cross until monitoring catches up.

Looping goes flagship. Paper 1 is the third looped-model story in four days, after Inkling 2 Mini's looped MoE on Nov 20 — but at 750B scale, looping is now a flagship strategy, not a small-model trick. Paper 2 is its governance mirror image: the first time a frontier lab has published its own internal capability curve and asked rivals to agree to a shared ceiling on it — with no numeric limit, no auditor and no enforcement body named.

Open weights, two ways. Paper 3, DiffuSpace's Clover-35B-A3B-Preview, is a diffusion MoE that vendor-reports 73.6 on SWE-bench Verified and 680 tokens/s on a single GB300 — under a non-commercial licence. Paper 4, Tencent's full Hy4 weights (770B total, 49B active, 1M context, an 82-page system card), are the largest fully open-weight multimodal flagship yet — but under the Hy License with a MaaS revenue cap, not Apache 2.0.

Memory, mixing, and what agent safety can promise. Paper 5, MemOnce, cuts 1M-context cache 1,313× (author-reported); paper 6, cmHC, topped Hugging Face Daily Papers with a geometric fix for hyper-connection stream mixing. Papers 7 and 8 form a pair: ROGUE-AI finds most real agent harm has no attacker at all, while Pandora's Toolbox proves agent safety is undecidable in general. Paper 9, Pro² Agent, sets a new MLE-Bench record at 76.0% by tracking the problem, not the code.

Lead sources linked below: LLMLearner and TestingCatalog on GLM-5.4 (secondary coverage), Reuters and TechTimes on the Pachocki essay (secondary coverage), arXiv abstract pages, deAI on the Hy4 weights, and the Nov 22 AI Agent Arxiv Digest.

⚠ Caveats — read before citing

  • Papers 1 and 2 rest on secondary coverage only. No primary Z.ai technical post on GLM-5.4 and no direct read of Pachocki's "Perplexity and Intelligence" essay were verified; both items are built from secondary reporting (LLMLearner, TestingCatalog, Reuters, TechTimes), flagged on the item.
  • Vendor- and author-reported numbers are labelled as such: GLM-5.4's "comparable to 2T–3T models" claim and ablation numbers (paper 1), Clover's benchmark scores and 680 tok/s (paper 3), Hy4's benchmark claims (paper 4), and MemOnce's 1,313× cache and 6.9× prefill figures (paper 5) come from the labs' or authors' own evaluations, not independent audits. Pachocki's perplexity figures (paper 2) are OpenAI's internal measurements on a private held-out code set.
  • Two briefs are unverified listings: Ant Group's LingSante-3.1-flash and Claude joining Google Meet calls rest on secondary roundup listings only, with no primary announcement verified — flagged on the brief.
  • Licences differ from the headlines: Clover's weights are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (research use only), and Hy4 ships under the Tencent Hy License v1.0 with a MaaS revenue cap — neither is Apache 2.0, and Hy4's August preview did ship under Apache 2.0 while this release does not.
  • No new frontier-model launch from OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta AI, or Mistral was found dated Nov 20–23; the week's big launches (Gemini 4 Neon, GPT-6.2 Astra/Sol, Qwen4-Max, Microsoft's MAI models, Claude Fable/Mythos 5.2) all landed Nov 16–19 and are not re-covered here.
  • arXiv papers are preprints, not peer-reviewed. Scores and claims are author-reported unless noted. ROGUE-AI's 89% reproduction rate comes from simulated environments and should not be read as a production risk rate.
