Wednesday edition · Open weights & fragile evidence

Mistral Large 4 goes open-weight as agents lose the thread — and the rulebook

A trillion-parameter open model lands the same day two evaluation papers show how easily agent evidence goes missing: compacted agents break rules they still hold, and a judge can score 60 steps without ever seeing a tool call.

Editor's note: A day of open weights and fragile evidence: Mistral's 1T-parameter flagship goes open-weight, MiniMax pitches a 1M-context coding agent, and the papers that matter most are about what agents and their judges fail to preserve — rules under compaction, tool calls in front of a judge, and findings that expire in about 16 months.

The weight-space story leads. GPT-6 Astra reportedly edits just 0.016% of a small model's weights to lift MATH-500 sixfold — treated here as a research post, not a paper, with its caveats front and centre.

The evaluation story is a pair. ARCBench shows compacted agents violate rules still in context 2.6× as often, while the input-assurance paper finds a judge scoring blank steps with matching hashes.

And the shelf-life warning: 44% of 50 widely cited LLM findings have expired on re-test, with a mean lifespan of 15.7 months.
