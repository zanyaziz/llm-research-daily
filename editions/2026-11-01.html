Sunday edition · Representation, agent memory & the evaluation reckoning

LeCun’s Platonic theorem: a 109M JEPA out-aligns 72B LLMs

Saturday’s papers argue the field has been measuring the wrong thing in three different places. A theorem from LeCun’s group says cross-modal alignment is about paired views, not scale; Meta’s FAIR team shows agent memory should be routed, not pooled; and a 91-paper audit finds most “hallucination” results are not measuring hallucinations at all. Add a benchmark that separates retrieval from rediscovery, and a judge-stability study showing leaderboard aggregates can look calm while individual verdicts churn — and this is an evaluation-reckoning edition, with one practical deployment brief (Bedrock GovCloud) and one migration deadline (OpenAI’s API retirements) alongside it.

The lead paper. Balestriero and LeCun turn the Platonic Representation Hypothesis into a theorem: JEPAs trained separately on any number of modalities learn the same representation, up to one rotation, whenever their positive pairs are two observations of one scene. Good views need 42 times fewer training samples than next-token prediction for equal alignment, and a 109M-parameter text JEPA aligns with DINOv2 and CLIP better than every language model tested up to 72B parameters.

The memory paper. FAIR at Meta’s Routed Continual Learning clusters an agent’s past trajectories and gives each cluster its own memory system, instead of one global history. Across four language models it raises normalized reward by 15–30% of the maximum attainable and cuts estimated API cost by 65–72% — while a single global history, on interleaved tasks, gains almost nothing over no memory at all (+0.015 vs routed +0.280).

Also today. A 91-paper audit finds only 35% of hallucination papers define hallucination as a confident error, and mitigations fix confabulations barely at all. OpenFrontiers separates genuine rediscovery from retrieval — closed-book, models rediscover at most 7 of 40. And an Arena-Hard judge study shows aggregates moving ~1 point while verdicts flip on 21.2% of prompts.
