Thursday edition · Decision models, post-training theory & benchmark measurement

Decision models arrive: Google's Gemini Decisions API leads a benchmark-measurement week

Google launches a model that doesn't generate text at all — it answers up to a thousand structured questions against one input in a single forward pass — while the day's papers converge on an uncomfortable theme: our training methods lose coverage, our agent memories mislead, and our leaderboards stop ranking long before the tasks run out.

A new model category. The Gemini Decisions API, powered by Gemini Decision 1, evaluates up to 1,000 yes/no, multiple-choice, and ordered-rubric questions against one shared multimodal input — text, images, audio, video, PDFs — returning calibrated probabilities and a confidence score for each, at a Google-reported 50–100 ms median latency and $0.10 per million input tokens with output tokens free. Google already uses it for routing in Gemini Notebook, design iteration in Stitch, and as a safety layer watching MCP tools in Search. It joins TypeSafe's Jev and OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna in a decision-model category that barely existed a month ago.

Post-training gets its theory paper. The day's top paper proves that standard post-training RL with a fixed reference policy suffers "coverage collapse" — it progressively loses the high-reward responses it needs to find — and offers periodic reference resets with the first global-convergence guarantees for the family. Two companion results sharpen the point: dense process rewards in GRPO lose once compute is matched, and a teacher that scaffolds RL training automatically beats outcome-only baselines at a third of the steps.

Measurement is the story. Three of the eight papers are about evaluation itself. ARE-bench shows research agents that pass ~69% of delivery checks pass only 28–40% once a project's history and workspace state count; a re-scoring of eight benchmarks argues saturation lives in the scoring, not the tasks; and MMDM — the natural companion benchmark to the Decisions launch — finds the best decision model recovers complete multi-select answers just 36.81% of the time. Add WikiTrace's finding that agent memory helped on only 18% of tasks, and the window's message is consistent: the field's instruments, not just its models, need work.

Lead sources linked in the sections below: Google (Gemini Decisions API); arXiv 2611.14467, 2611.14320, 2611.14364, 2611.13541, 2611.14309.
