Saturday edition · Self-modifying models, architecture at scale & the week's biggest open-weights drop

Models that rewrite their own weights — and a looped 26B that runs on a laptop

Friday's headline result collapses the wall between training and inference: an open 27B model, given its own weights and its graded answers, diagnoses its failures and edits the weights behind them — no gradients, no training — gaining 6.6 points on unseen problems, with gains that compound over eight self-editing sessions. Alongside it, Zeyuan Allen-Zhu's 64-run pretraining study shows benchmarks hiding real architectural weaknesses, a single-author paper compiles a CPU directly into pretrained weights, and Thinking Machines' looped Inkling 2 Mini puts frontier-class agentic coding on a 32GB laptop.

A model that edits itself. Paper 1 (Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights) is the first demonstration of a language model rewriting its own weights by its own reasoning and becoming better for it. The model can even repair a damaged copy of itself back to over 80% of original accuracy on unseen image tasks without ever reading the original weights — and the ability emerges only at scale: smaller models in the same family make no useful edit, or break themselves.

Architecture, measured honestly. Paper 2 (Physics of Language Models, Part 4.2) runs 64 from-scratch pretraining runs at up to 2 trillion tokens and finds “Canon” layers save ~30% of pretraining tokens for the same MMLU accuracy at 8B — while linear models that match Transformers on benchmarks still cannot reliably retrieve from a 500-token context, and no model beats random guessing on the simplest two-hop questions. Paper 3 compiles exact arithmetic circuits directly into pretrained weights with zero gradient steps, reaching 100% ALU-prefix accuracy where LoRA fine-tuning stalls.

The open-weights story. In the briefs, Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini (Nov 20): a 26.4B-total / 3.4B-active looped transformer that reuses its middle layers three times per token, ships under Apache 2.0 in a ~20GB checkpoint, and — by the vendor's reported numbers — leads all open models under 100B on agentic coding. No new frontier-model launch from the big labs landed in the Nov 19–20 window; the briefs rest on secondary coverage and are flagged as such.

Lead sources linked below: arXiv 2611.21885 (Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights), 2611.24963 (Physics of Language Models 4.2), 2611.26450 (A CPU Inside an LLM), The Decoder and MarkTechPost coverage of Inkling 2 Mini (secondary).
