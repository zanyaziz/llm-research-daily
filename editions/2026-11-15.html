Sunday edition · Robotics, reorganised & decision models that teach

OpenAI bets general models can run robots — and decision models that teach better than they act

No new frontier model release on a moderately busy Saturday, but the week's big theme is organizational: OpenAI folded its standalone robotics team on the bet that general models like GPT-6 Astra can run robots directly — the same week Runway open-weighted its specialist robot model Praxis-1. Anthropic took Claude Managed Agents self-hosted, llama.cpp hit 1.0.0, LMSYS formed the SGLang Consortium, and a strong agent-focused paper crop rethinks how small decision models act, judge, and teach.

Robotics, two opposite bets. Fortune reported Nov 13 that OpenAI disbanded its standalone robotics team, moving researchers into main research under Mark Chen — on the stated view that general-purpose models such as GPT-6 Astra can now be applied to robots directly. Days earlier Runway took the opposite route, open-weighting Praxis-1, a 27B specialist world-action model. Both stories lean on the same simulation benchmark, RoboLab-120, with vendor/press-reported figures that are not independently verified — flagged as such below.

Infrastructure goes formal. Anthropic opened a self-hosted private beta for Claude Managed Agents, llama.cpp and ggml reached v1.0.0 with a stable C API promise, and LMSYS launched the SGLang Consortium with eight founding members spanning rival chipmakers. The open inference stack is acquiring governance, versioning and enterprise deployment forms.

The paper theme: small models, better roles. The top paper shows decision models are mediocre actors but excellent judges in hindsight — and, as teachers, lift a 1.5B policy by 10–15 points. A companion paper asks when thinking is worth paying for at all, cutting latency 72.5% by routing. Memory, physics-aware video and subliminal learning in VLMs round out a crop heavy on agents and honest about its caveats.

Lead sources linked in the sections below: Fortune reporting via explainx.ai and DailyMech; LMSYS (primary); arXiv 2611.17167, 2611.16961, 2611.16748, 2611.10439.
