AI for science · Protein design

GPT-6 designs proteins that bind — in a real wet lab, not just on a benchmark

OpenAI reports that its GPT-6 family can design new protein structures and amino-acid sequences using only general-purpose reasoning and code, without calling specialized biology models to generate the designs — and in wet-lab tests with partner Onava, 35 of 322 AI-designed “minibinders” showed evidence of binding across five disease-relevant targets. Also today: an Axios investigation into whether unreleased frontier models can be released safely at all, a benchmark showing top agents fail a third of everyday tasks, and Anthropic turning MCP into a distribution channel for agent skills.

Wet-lab evidence, not a score. The 35 binding minibinders (10.9% of 322) hit five disease-relevant targets: PD-L1, IL-7Rα, TrkA, VEGF-A, and IL-17A. An internal research model matched or beat specialized models on many of 14 protein tasks — though full protein structure prediction remains a clear weakness.

The limits, in OpenAI's own telling. The workflow still used specialized models (Boltz-2, ESMFold2) to filter candidates, so end-to-end zero-shot design is not yet demonstrated. The results are vendor-reported — OpenAI's own blog post and companion preprint — and have not been independently reproduced.
