Benchmarks & evaluation

Top agents fail a third of everyday tasks — and the same easy exam twice is too much to ask

Two benchmarks landed on November 24 and both point the same way: frontier models are far less reliable than their headline scores suggest. ShellBench put agents on 100 everyday chores; DeepMind's Humanity's First Exam asked models to solve the same simple task four slightly different ways. Also today: a Transformer that rewires its own circuits, harness training that generalizes to tasks 64x longer, and two papers showing the evaluation stack itself is wobbly.

Everyday chores, a third unfinished. ShellBench (OpenClaw Foundation) built 100 everyday agent tasks from 10M+ anonymized real-world interactions. The best agent, GPT-6 Astra, completed 62.5%; Claude Fable 5.2 scored 61.9% and GPT-6.1 Sol 61.4%. 15.7% of attempts failed at least one safety requirement, and information omission caused 51% of failures. The harness mattered as much as the model — Meta's Muse Wonder scored 56% in the Pi harness but 38.1% in Claude Code.

The same task, four ways. Humanity's First Exam (Google DeepMind) is a 544-prompt benchmark in 136 four-variant families across 20 foundational capabilities. Under family pass^4 scoring — all four variants must pass — the top five frontier models average 85.6%, versus 96–98% single-shot accuracy. GPT-6.2 Astra leads the public leaderboard at 90.7%; about 74% of errors are image-interpretation failures. It is live as a Kaggle competition through December 15, 2026.
