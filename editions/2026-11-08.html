Sunday edition · Cryptanalysis, agent memory & frontier releases

DeepMind's AI agents find cracks in legacy RSA — as open-weight giants and a 'Critical'-rated cyber model reshape the week

Google DeepMind announced on Nov 6 that human-supervised AI agents produced two cryptanalysis advances against legacy 1024-bit RSA in a matter of weeks: a signature-forgery method that strips PKCS#1 v1.5 padding of its protection, and “fan factories,” a batch-factoring method that makes each additional legacy key far cheaper to break. Around it, a Friday-heavy week: Anthropic published its design doctrine for agent memory and “dreaming,” Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Small made the strongest US open-weight claim to date, and OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber became the first frontier model rated “Critical” for offensive cyber capability.

The forgery result. The agents showed that PKCS#1 v1.5 padding adds no protection once an attacker controls more than half of the signed block. Reproduced on a solved benchmark key, the attack took 391 core-years against 1,380 originally — 3.5x less compute — with a theoretical ~1,600x improvement estimated for short 32-byte digests. The work is led by Krishnamurthy (Dj) Dvijotham and Abhradeep Thakurta, with Nadia Heninger (UC San Diego) and Emmanuel Thomé (Inria).

The factoring result. “Fan factories” is a batch-factoring method: after a shared precomputation, each additional 1024-bit key costs roughly 1/58 of a full factorization — about 30x cheaper than the best prior batch method. The two working papers are “Fan Factories: Multiple Number Fields in Factorization Factories and Batch NFS” and “Modular Roots from a Restricted Oracle,” with key identities machine-checked in Lean 4.

Why it matters. This is the first high-profile case of AI agents doing novel, verifiable cryptanalysis alongside human experts, compressing years of work into weeks — and it sharpens the case for retiring legacy 1024-bit keys. DeepMind stresses that modern RSA-2048 and above is unaffected, and that the agents worked under human supervision.

Read the caveats before citing. The ~1,600x and ~30x figures are theoretical estimates, not executed computations; the factoring method has no implementation yet. The story is verified through secondary coverage so far — the DeepMind announcement (Pushmeet Kohli, VP of Science, on X, Nov 6) is cited by title below.
