Open-weights avalanche: Hy4, Ripple-65B and Kimi K3.1 Pro land as OpenAI's pipeline finds 948 root exploits

November 4 was driven by model releases rather than papers: three major open-weight events in one day, OpenAI's autonomous Linux-kernel audit, and Anthropic's cryptographic proof of which model actually answered — while the pure-paper crop warned that agentic RL gains are more budget-dependent than the headlines suggest.

Editor's note: A strong day, driven by model releases rather than papers. November 4 delivered three major open-weight events — Tencent's Hy4, Ricursive's Ripple-65B and Kimi K3.1 Pro's weights — plus OpenAI's striking Linux-kernel security result and Anthropic's FlashProve. The pure-paper crop was solid but not exceptional: routing, agentic RL and reasoning-topology papers led.

The open-weights story comes with the edition's biggest caveat. Tencent's Hy4 weights were promised, not published, on launch day — until they ship, Hy4 is API-only. Kimi K3.1 Pro is the opposite case: Moonshot met its promised Nov 4 date and the ~1.56 TB of weights are actually downloadable.

The security story is a pair. OpenAI's pipeline produced 948 distinct Linux kernel vulnerabilities in one run, and the week's top safety paper shows a pretraining-poison backdoor surviving clean SFT, DPO and RL essentially untouched.

And the caution from the papers: multi-turn RL gains depend heavily on sufficient turn budgets and diminish sharply when budgets tighten — a directly actionable warning for anyone training or deploying agents under tight turn limits.

⚠ Caveats — how to read this edition

  • Biggest caveat — Hy4 weights: Tencent's "open-weight" status is promised, not delivered: the full-release weights were not published on launch day, only promised "in the coming days" alongside a system card. Until they ship, Hy4 is API-only.
  • True dates: items carry their true dates — Route Anything is 3 Nov, FlashProve and the reasoning-topology paper are 2 Nov (FlashProve coverage 2–3 Nov), and the agentic-backdoor paper was submitted 27 Oct and is included as late-breaking because it hit Hacker News' front page on 2 Nov. The multi-turn RL, Dory and Axion papers and the OpenAI kernel post are 4 Nov items.
  • Vendor- and author-reported: all benchmark figures are vendor- or author-reported and not independently verified unless noted — including Ripple-65B's coding scores and ~$32M cost claim, Hy4's benchmark figures (its two English announcements even disagree on hallucination-rate figures), and Axion's HLE and FrontierScience scores.
  • OpenAI's own account: every figure in the kernel-exploit item is OpenAI's own account of a controlled prototype run; the exploits were not published and nothing was independently verified.
  • Prototype only: FlashProve is an open-source research prototype — Anthropic is explicit that it is not maintained and there are no deployment plans.
  • Checked and excluded: "Post-Training Leaves Behavioral Shadows on Unrelated Decisions" could not be verified from a primary source in this research pass and was excluded rather than reported unverified. The Catherine Wu move (reported 5 Nov) sits outside the Nov 3–4 window and appears in briefs as context only.
