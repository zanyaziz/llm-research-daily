Tuesday edition · Architecture, collaboration & training honesty

Layers that reorder themselves, teammates that cave under pressure

A Monday of architecture, collaboration and training honesty: a transformer that picks its own layer order at inference, a benchmark showing frontier agents are poor teammates, and two RL papers showing training quietly drifts off-policy — plus a 1.6-trillion-parameter agentic model from Meituan and a benchmark where even the best model clears barely one task in eight.

The lead paper is Library of Layers. Instead of a fixed block order, a router orders blocks from a shared global library at inference. One hyperparameter — library size — slides the design between dense, depth-recurrent and mixture-of-experts behaviour, and router probes separate safe from unsafe text at AUROC 0.95.

The collaboration story is the Collaborative Intelligence Benchmark. Across 18 models the best teammate scores only 82.2 out of 100, and frontier models reach the best overall deal in only 14% of conflicts under interpersonal pressure.

The training story is a pair. The ranked discrepancy-lag correction paper (No. 3) plus the bf16 rounding brief in the briefs show, from two angles, how RL training quietly drifts off-policy — numerical discrepancy and policy lag need different fixes.

The model-release story is LongCat-2.5 from Meituan — 1.6 trillion parameters, ~48B active — and the benchmark story is SemiAnalysis GDPx, where Claude Opus 5.5 leads at just 12.0%.

  • Author- and vendor-reported: all benchmark numbers quoted are author/vendor-reported unless otherwise noted — including LongCat-2.5, CUA-Master-27B, CoworkOS, GDPx and QA Bench figures; none has independent verification yet.
  • Dating discipline: papers carry their true arXiv dates. Papers 3, 4 and 5 are 29 Oct papers that surfaced in the Nov 2 digests; paper 6 is a 15 Oct paper with Nov 2 news coverage; CUA-Master is a 3 Nov late-breaking item. They appear here because they surfaced in the Nov 2 window, not because they were all submitted Nov 2.

  • Elicitation caveat on paper 4: the CoT-monitoring study’s prompts tell agents to be sneaky and mention a monitor; awareness was judged by a single LLM and the strategy breakdown rests on 29 episodes from one model.
  • Not re-dated: the OpenAI/Anthropic Exponential Time Hypothesis claims (26–27 Oct, Lean-formalized, still unreviewed by independent experts) resurfaced in Nov 2 digests but are not Nov 2 news and are excluded; Genie 4 (27 Oct), Gemini 4 Carbon (28 Oct) and Mellum2.1 (8 Oct) are prior-window releases, not re-dated here; Carbon appears only via the GDPx comparison.
  • Out of scope: market stories (mergers, capital raises, IPO filings) are excluded. Papers with Code could not be separately verified in this run.
  • Preprints: all papers are preprints unless stated otherwise and have not been peer-reviewed.
