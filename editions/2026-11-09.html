Monday edition · Cognitive symbionts, metric blind spots & the open-weight scoreboard

Augment, don't replace: Oxford's 'cognitive symbiont' tops the week's papers as agents' real-task ceiling stays low

Hao Fei's 59-page position paper argues the field is aiming at the wrong endgame — AI-augmented human intelligence, not autonomous AGI — and it topped Hugging Face Daily Papers with 120 upvotes. Around it, a sobering weekend: the best coding agent fully finishes only 26.4% of real tasks on Co-Design Bench, automatic metrics score a complete translation failure as perfect, and the open-weight scoreboard now has a clear leader in Kimi K3.1 Pro.

The symbiont argument. Fei (University of Oxford) proposes the 'cognitive symbiont': a lifelong, embedded, real-time, private, low-friction, proactive companion that senses what a person senses through external devices, keeps their context across years, models their situation and their mind, takes part in their thinking at the right moment, and keeps learning the person as both change. The design space is organized as a six-stage symbiosis loop — sensing, contextual retention, understanding, task execution, collaborative thinking, and continuous learning — built around the 'cognitive gap' between what a person could bring to a situation and what they can hold in working memory. A companion project page is live at cognitive-symbiont.github.io.

The metric failure. In 'The Invisible Gap' (arXiv:2611.09085, submitted Nov 6), four frontier models — GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 2.5-flash and Qwen3.5-35B — rendered 142 English query-response pairs into romanized Hindi, Telugu and Kannada. Embedding metric BGE-M3 separates GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8 by just 0.004 (0.790 vs 0.786), while native speakers separate them by 1.43 of 12 points — and English returned verbatim, a complete task failure, scores BGE 1.000 and BERTScore-F1 1.000.

Why it matters. The two stories rhyme: both are about evaluation failing to see what matters — whether a system actually augments a person, and whether a translation actually happened. Fei's paper is a position piece, not an experiment, and the romanized-Indic study rests on one rater per language and 72 human-scored responses — read both with the caveats in this edition.

Lead story sources: arXiv:2611.05376 (Hao Fei) and arXiv:2611.09085 (Sharma et al.) — links in the Top papers section.
