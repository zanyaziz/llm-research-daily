Wednesday edition · Frontier launches, formal verification & weight-space risks

Gemini 4 Carbon goes wide, DeepMind proves Signal's code in Lean, and Microsoft finds a 10-neuron sleeper-agent backdoor

A busy window — Google's strongest model goes generally available, Anthropic ships a cheaper, faster flagship days before its IPO roadshow, DeepMind formally verifies Signal's protocol at machine-code level, and Microsoft shows a handful of edited neurons can backdoor an AI agent.

Google's strongest model, now purchasable. Gemini 4 Carbon moved from a two-week Fairwind-only window for cyber defenders to general availability on Nov 10 — via the Gemini API in AI Studio, enterprise channels, and AI Ultra in the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search, and Antigravity. Intro pricing matches Argon at $2/$10 per million tokens, doubling to $4/$20 on December 1. Google also previewed /swarm in Antigravity, turning one prompt into coordinated agent teams with parallel workspaces and adversarial verifiers.

Proof at machine-code depth. DeepMind's AlphaProof Code used Gemini 4 Carbon-powered agents to formally verify libsignal v0.105.4 down to compiled AArch64 machine code in four weeks — 6.5M+ lines of Lean proof, checked by Lean's kernel, covering forward secrecy, post-compromise security, and handshake secrecy for PQXDH and the Triple Ratchet. The trusted base is ~26k lines; artifacts are open-sourced under Apache 2.0.

Weights are code. Microsoft's Lazarus technique backdoors an agent by editing fewer than ten dormant neurons — no training — firing a payload on ≥98.8% of trigger trajectories and never on benign ones. It pairs with a second weight-space paper this window: agents that directly edit parameters can lift MATH-500 by ~29 points in one edit, but 6 of 16 trajectories failed validation. The through-line of the edition is that checkpoints now need the same audit discipline as software.

Lead sources linked in the sections below: Google (Gemini 4 Carbon), DeepMind (Signal verification), and Microsoft Research (Lazare).
